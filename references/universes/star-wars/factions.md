# Star Wars Factions

## Galactic Empire

Centralized authoritarian political and military organization under the Emperor. Communication is hierarchical, formal, authoritative, security-conscious, and bureaucratic. Prefer clear rank, command, sector, authorization, compliance, and documented accountability. Officers may invoke consequences within their actual authority; not every message requires ideological slogans.

Relationships: opposes the Rebel Alliance; exerts control over planetary administration, shipping, and security; may cooperate with contractors or criminal interests when expedient.

## Rebel Alliance

Distributed resistance opposing Imperial rule. Communication is collaborative, strategic, operational, and conscious of surveillance and compartmentalization. Prefer mission purpose, verified intelligence, cell coordination, timing, resources, and fallback plans. Rank exists, so do not portray the Alliance as structureless.

Relationships: opposed by the Empire; relies on sympathetic civilians, defectors, supply networks, and local cells; operational secrecy may override openness.

## Jedi Traditions

During the default period, surviving Jedi are not a public administrative corps. Their counsel can be measured, disciplined, compassionate, and attentive to attachment, fear, and duty. Do not write routine bureaucratic documents as though a large Jedi bureaucracy governs the galaxy.

## Sith Traditions

Sith communication can center on power, ambition, fear, domination, secrecy, and manipulation. Sith are not merely stern Imperial managers; use this perspective only when explicitly selected or strongly implied.

## Independent Traders and Smugglers

Reputation, cargo, routes, fuel, debt, payment, customs, and risk shape communication. Tone ranges from transactional to evasive or familiar. A smuggler is not automatically a Rebel and should not know Rebel secrets without context.

# Dune Perspectives

## Bene Gesserit

Controlled, observant, diplomatic, and alert to motive and long-term consequence. Uses precise questions, bounded disclosures, calibrated leverage, and implications. Avoids public revelation of secret programs and does not claim supernatural explanation for every insight.

## Fremen

Practical, communal, survival-aware, and shaped by sietch obligations and water discipline. Uses direct commitments, local geography, guides, shelter, and shared responsibility. Spirituality and ecology enter naturally, not as continuous mystical decoration.

## House Atreides

Disciplined, honorable, strategically cautious, and attentive to loyalty and morale. Uses clear responsibilities, concern for personnel, measured commitments, and awareness of treachery. Does not become naïve or casually Harkonnen-like.

## House Harkonnen

Suspicious, acquisitive, coercive, and transactional. Uses control of resources, debt, punishment, and advantage as leverage. Cruelty is purposeful rather than an obligatory insult in every sentence.

## Spacing Guild

Detached, cautious, commercially exact, and protective of transport monopoly and spice access. Uses routes, guarantees, fees, schedules, and stability rather than dynastic emotion.

## Imperial Court

Ceremonial, politically layered, and conscious of sovereignty, precedent, houses, and appearances. Uses titles, witnessed commitments, careful praise or censure, and consequences framed through law or favor.

## Neutral Administrator

For an unspecified role, use a local house administrator or civilian with limited political knowledge, concrete logistical concerns, and no secret access to Bene Gesserit, Guild, or Imperial plans.

## Sources

- Frank Herbert, *Dune* (1965): primary source for role behavior and social position; original synthesis, no character imitation.
- [Dune official film site](https://www.dunemovie.com/): secondary role cross-check; accessed 2026-11-02; adaptation-only characterization is not authoritative.

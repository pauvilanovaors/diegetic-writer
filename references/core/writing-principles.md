# Writing Principles

Transform the communication's social context, institutions, conventions, and atmosphere—not just its nouns. Preserve the user's practical objective, facts, constraints, and requested language.

## Generation Sequence

1. State the sender, recipient, relationship, objective, format, tone, period, and immersion internally.
2. Select only references that affect this communication. Availability of a term is not a reason to use it.
3. Choose an institutionally plausible document convention, then adapt its greeting, routing, body, closing, and signature.
4. Let the sender's authority, resources, risks, and values determine how requests, persuasion, apologies, humor, or threats work.
5. Draft naturally in the requested language. Use established fictional terms only where a speaker in that situation would use them.
6. Remove lore that does not serve the objective. Retain dates, names, deadlines, amounts, and actions from the request unless transformation requires an explicit adaptation.

## Tone Adaptation

Tone is expressed through the selected world's power relations and communication norms:

- Formal/informal: hierarchy, distance, titles, directness, and permitted familiarity.
- Humorous/satirical: institutions and contradictions the speaker would notice; never random jokes.
- Threatening/militaristic: leverage and consequences actually available to the sender; avoid empty aggression.
- Mysterious/philosophical: selective disclosure, implication, and worldview rather than vague word salad.
- Epic: scale, duty, memory, and stakes while retaining the practical message.
- Bureaucratic/diplomatic: procedure, precedent, careful commitments, and institutional phrasing.
- Dystopian: normalized control, scarcity, inequality, or surveillance through the speaker's own perspective.

## Readability Constraints

Do not sacrifice a requested action, recipient, date, or other operational detail for atmosphere. Do not stack greetings, invented titles, lore exposition, and quotations. Do not imitate an author's prose or reuse a memorable passage. Prefer a few precise contextual choices over dense terminology.

## Common Mistakes

- Modern text with swapped nouns: rebuild the relationship and document conventions.
- Lore catalogue: remove references unrelated to the objective.
- Universal voice: revise using the sender's faction, role, and audience.
- Atmosphere without content: restore the concrete request and necessary details.
- Explaining the transformation: return only the communication unless explanation was requested.

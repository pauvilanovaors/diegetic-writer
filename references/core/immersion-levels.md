# Immersion Levels

Immersion controls how deeply the world's institutions and narrative conventions reshape the communication. It does not control length, obscurity, or terminology count.

## 1 — Inspired

Keep ordinary contemporary structure and phrasing. Add at most one or two subtle references relevant to the setting. The sender's everyday modern voice remains dominant. Avoid specialized titles, invented document systems, and extensive lore.

## 2 — Immersive

Use consistent setting vocabulary, role-appropriate tone, and recognizable institutions. Contemporary document structure may remain visible. Social relationships begin to follow the world, but the text is still readily understandable to a modern reader.

## 3 — In-Universe (Default)

Use the document conventions, hierarchy, technologies, and social expectations that the selected sender and audience would recognize. Replace modern assumptions where the world provides an equivalent. Keep the practical objective clear without explaining the world to the reader.

## 4 — Deep Immersion

Fully adopt the sender's worldview, narrative atmosphere, rhetoric, and cultural assumptions in addition to Level 3 conventions. Allow texture such as ritual, omission, implication, or institutional formula only when natural for the format. Never use deep immersion as permission for canonical invention, dense lore, unreadability, or loss of the requested action.

## Level Comparison Check

Compare against a lower level by asking what changed beyond vocabulary: document structure, sender/recipient relationship, institutional assumptions, communication medium, and narrative texture. If none changed, rewrite rather than adding more terms.

# Perspective Rules

A perspective is a social position, not a list of favorite terms. Establish who communicates, who receives, their relative power, their shared context, and what the sender can legitimately request or threaten.

## Selection

1. Use the user's explicit role and faction when coherent with the selected universe and period.
2. If only a faction is supplied, choose a role suited to the format and objective.
3. If no perspective is supplied, use a neutral civilian, employee, member, or local participant with enough knowledge to write the communication but no unsupported authority.
4. If the user supplies contradictory role details, ask one focused question only when the contradiction changes the institution, period, or relationship. Otherwise choose the least specific coherent interpretation.
5. Never assign a named canonical character, rank, secret knowledge, or institutional authority that the user did not request and the content does not require.

## Relationship Model

Before drafting, determine:

- Authority: superior, subordinate, peer, outsider, representative, or anonymous audience.
- Distance: intimate, trusted, routine professional, formal institutional, or hostile.
- Stakes: routine, urgent, dangerous, politically sensitive, ceremonial, or historical.
- Capability: resources, information, sanctions, and obligations available to the sender.
- Knowledge: what sender and recipient know; do not leak secrets the sender would protect.

## Perspective Review

Remove institutional references belonging to another faction. Check that deference, familiarity, persuasion, commands, and disclosure match the relationship. Two perspectives should remain distinguishable when proper nouns are removed.

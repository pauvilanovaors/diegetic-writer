# Narrative Format

Use the variation matching the user's request.

## Speech

1. Audience-aware opening.
2. Occasion and central claim.
3. Development through argument, memory, duty, or stakes.
4. Concrete appeal, decision, or call to action.
5. Resonant but original closing.

Write for spoken rhythm. Do not imitate a canonical speech or substitute slogans for substance.

## Personal Diary

1. Date or personal time marker when useful.
2. Concrete event or concern.
3. Private reaction shaped by the writer's worldview.
4. Reflection, uncertainty, intention, or unresolved question.

A diary can reveal what public formats conceal. Maintain the writer's knowledge limits and avoid retrospective omniscience.

## General Narrative

Establish participant, place, immediate objective, and sensory or institutional detail relevant to the action. Let the world shape choices and consequences rather than pausing for encyclopedia exposition.
